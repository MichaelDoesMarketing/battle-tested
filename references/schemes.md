# Schemes

Read this during Round 3. A scheme is one concept that generates many lines. In a battle it is a run of bars built on a single frame. In marketing it is the campaign concept that makes a homepage, an ad set, a sales deck, and a conference booth sound like one thing.

One clever line is a moment. A scheme is a system, and it is why some brands sound consistent without a style guide nobody reads.

## How to build one

**Pick a domain the audience already knows cold.** Not one you find interesting. The scheme borrows the audience's existing knowledge, so the borrowing has to be free. Sports, cooking, weather, traffic, dating, construction, medicine, whatever they already live in.

**Run the mechanism through it, not the vibe.** Weak schemes decorate. Strong ones map structure to structure. If your product removes a bottleneck, the domain should have bottlenecks with names, causes, and consequences you can borrow.

**Generate ten lines from the frame before committing.** If the frame only produces two, it is a metaphor, not a scheme. Kill it and pick another.

**Check the second meaning.** Every domain drags associations with it. A war frame on a healthcare product reads differently than it does on a security product. Say the frame out loud with the buyer in the room before you build a quarter on it.

## The jargon flip

The strongest scheme move in marketing: take the category's own vocabulary and run it through an outside frame, or take an outside vocabulary and run it through the category. The tension does the work, and it makes internal jargon suddenly legible to outsiders.

It also gives you a repeatable engine. Once the flip is set, every new asset writes itself in the same voice without repeating the same line.

Use it when the message needs a hook and the product itself is not visually or functionally novel.

## Running the scheme across assets

Set the frame once, at the highest surface, usually the hero line or the campaign name. After that, each asset uses one branch of the frame, never the whole thing. Repeating the full frame everywhere turns the campaign into a mascot.

Order of build:
1. Campaign line - the frame stated once, cleanest
2. Three to five sub-lines - branches for the main surfaces
3. Proof mapped to each branch - a receipt behind every claim the frame makes
4. The exit - what the frame says about the buyer's decision, not about the product

## When to kill it

Kill the scheme when the frame starts driving the message instead of carrying it. Symptoms: you are inventing product claims to fit the metaphor, the sub-lines need explanation, internal people love it more than customers do, or the sales team stops using it in real conversations. That last one is the real signal. Sales says out loud what actually works.
