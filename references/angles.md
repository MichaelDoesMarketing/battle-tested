# Angles

Read this during Round 2. The goal is one angle, stated in one sentence.

## The hunt

Answer these before reaching for an archetype. The angle usually falls out of the answers rather than out of the list.

1. What does everyone in this category say? Write the sentence they all share. Your angle lives outside it.
2. What does the audience already believe that is quietly wrong, or right for the wrong reason?
3. What does the competitor's strength cost their customer? Every strength has a bill attached and nobody sends it.
4. What does the user know from doing the work that an outsider would not guess? Insider knowledge is the cheapest angle source and the most underused.
5. What is the thing the user is slightly nervous to say out loud? That reaction usually means it is true and unclaimed.
6. What would the customer have to admit for this product to be the obvious choice? That admission is often the angle.

## The archetypes

**The reframe.** Change the axis of comparison. If you are losing on price, the axis is not price. If everyone competes on features, compete on what happens in month six.

**The obvious secret.** Say the thing everyone in the room already knows and has agreed not to mention. Works because recognition lands faster than persuasion. The audience does not have to be convinced, only relieved.

**The receipt.** A proof so specific a competitor cannot copy it without doing the work. A number, a name, a timestamp, a failure you survived. Specificity is the only claim that cannot be commoditized.

**The scope narrow.** Their claim is true, but only inside a box smaller than the buyer thinks. You do not call them liars. You draw the box. "It does work. On clean data." This is the most defensible attack because you are conceding to win.

**The switch-up.** Take their headline strength and show it as the source of the buyer's problem. Enterprise grade means a six month implementation. Fully customizable means nobody on your team knows how it works.

**The uncomfortable question.** One question the buyer cannot answer without moving toward you. Do not answer it in the copy. An unanswered question follows people out of the room.

**The name.** Give the buyer's unnamed problem a name they will repeat. Once a thing has a name, it is a problem people can search, complain about, and shop for. Naming the problem is how you own the category before you own the market.

## Testing an angle

- Could a competitor say this exact sentence without lying? If yes, it is not an angle.
- Does it survive the buyer asking "so what" twice?
- Would someone repeat it to a colleague without you in the room? That is the whole test of whether it travels.
- Is it true enough that you would say it directly to the competitor's face at a conference? If not, it is a shot, not an angle, and shots without truth lose the round.

## Common failure

Picking three angles and shipping all of them. A message with three angles has none. Rank them, take the strongest, and keep the other two for a different asset or a later campaign.
