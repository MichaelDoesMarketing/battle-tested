# The crowd test

Read this during Round 6. A crowd does not evaluate a bar, it reacts or it does not. Scoring is how you approximate that reaction before the market gives it to you for free and expensively.

Score each surviving line 1 to 5 on five dimensions. Be a hard grader. A 3 is average marketing copy, which is the thing being replaced.

**Truth.** Can the user back it, today, with something a skeptic would accept. Anything at 3 or below is dead on the spot regardless of the other scores.

**Specificity.** Concrete nouns, real numbers, named situations. Category language scores 1. If the line would work for a competitor with the logo swapped, it scores 2 at best.

**Compression.** Could a word come out without losing meaning. Count the words that are doing nothing. Each one costs a point.

**Repeatability.** Would someone say this to a colleague from memory. Test by reading it once, looking away, and writing it down.

**Room fit.** Does the register match the buyer and the surface. A line can be excellent and still be wrong for a procurement review.

Total out of 25. Ship 20 and up. Rework 15 to 19. Kill below 15.

## Fast tests

**The swap test.** Put the competitor's name on the line. Still true and still fine? The line belongs to the category, not to the user. Cut it.

**The out loud test.** Read it at conversation speed. Any stumble means the rhythm is broken. Readers hit the same break silently and lose the line.

**The screenshot test.** Would anyone screenshot this and send it to one person. Not share it publicly, send it to one specific person. That is the real threshold for a line traveling.

**The stranger test.** Would someone outside the industry understand what is being sold within one read. Jargon that needs a translator loses everyone in the room except the four people who already agree with you.

**The response test.** Write the competitor's best comeback to the line. If it is easy to write and it is good, the line is exposed. Fix the line, not the competitor.

## Cutting

Say what got cut and why, briefly. The cuts teach faster than the keepers, and they show the work behind a short deliverable so three lines do not look like thirty seconds of effort.

Never pad a deliverable with lines that scored badly just to hit a number the user asked for. If they asked for ten and four are real, give four and say what happened to the rest.
