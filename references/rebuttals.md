# Rebuttals

Read this during Round 5. In a battle, a rebuttal answers what the opponent just said, live, in front of the crowd. In marketing you never get that turn, so you take it in advance.

The reader argues with you the whole time they read. Every objection you leave unanswered, they win by default, and they leave the page having beaten you silently.

## Pre-emption

Name the objection in the reader's own words, before they finish forming it, then answer it with a receipt.

Their words matter more than the answer. When a reader sees their exact hesitation printed on the page, the copy stops feeling like a pitch and starts feeling like someone who has done this before. That recognition buys you the next paragraph.

Wrong: "Some may wonder about implementation timelines."
Right: "You are thinking this takes six months and a consultant. It takes a Tuesday."

Sources for their exact words: sales call recordings, churn surveys, support tickets, review sites, the competitor's own review sites, and the questions the founder is tired of answering.

## The four objections

Almost everything collapses into these. Rank them by what actually kills deals for this product, not by what is easiest to answer.

**Price.** Rarely about the number. Usually about the buyer's confidence that the value shows up. Answer with a comparison to what they already spend on the problem, or with the cost of the status quo, dated and specific.

**Trust.** Do you exist next year, does it work at my scale, has anyone like me done this. Answer with names, numbers, and boring operational detail. Boring detail is the most persuasive proof there is, because nobody fakes it.

**Effort.** Switching cost, learning curve, internal politics, migration. This kills more deals than price and gets the least copy. Answer with the specific first hour: what happens, who does it, how long.

**Status quo.** The strongest competitor in almost every category is doing nothing. Doing nothing has no sales team and never gets attacked. Attack it directly, with the cost of another quarter unchanged.

## Receipts

A rebuttal without a receipt is just a louder claim. Ranked by strength:

1. A number with a source and a date
2. A named customer with a specific outcome
3. A demonstration the reader can run themselves in under five minutes
4. A guarantee with teeth, stated in what happens rather than in what is promised
5. An admission of a real limitation, which buys credibility for everything around it

That last one is underrated. Saying who the product is wrong for is the fastest way to be believed by the people it is right for.

## Placement

Put each rebuttal where the objection actually forms, not in a FAQ at the bottom. Price objections form at the price. Effort objections form at the demo request. A FAQ section is where objections go to be avoided politely.

Two or three rebuttals on a page. Answering nine reads like a defense, and a defense signals there is something to defend.

## The concession

Concede the small point to win the big one. "Yes, it is more expensive. Here is what you stop paying for." Conceding costs nothing when the concession is already obvious to the reader, and it makes the next claim land as fact instead of as sales.
